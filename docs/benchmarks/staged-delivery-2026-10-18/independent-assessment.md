## Separate Verdicts

- **Factual: material source-grounding failures.** Nine assertions across eight formats make unsupported objective operational, prevalence or categorical-limitation claims. This means unsupported by the brief, not externally proven false.
- **Editorial: 15/20 overall** (relevance 4, naturalness 3, originality 2, platform fit 3, usefulness 3). Relevant and readable, but repetitive and insufficiently polished. Scores do not certify factual safety.

## Material Findings

The source establishes: “A stock record describes what is held; a display note describes how an item is presented.” It does not establish the following outcomes or generalizations:

- **LinkedIn, body:** “It keeps details stable as items move through receiving, counting, replenishment, and reporting.” This adds an unqualified data-stability assurance across operational stages.
- **X post, body:** “Keeping them separate protects accuracy and makes the shop floor easier to run.” The accuracy protection claim exceeds the supplied distinction.
- **Facebook, body:** “In a museum shop, it helps to separate two things that often get blended together.” The brief does not establish that blending is common.
- **X thread, item 1:** “1) If your stock record is also trying to be a display guide, it usually ends up doing neither job well.” No evidence supports this frequency/performance claim.
- **Carousel, slide 7:** “Slide 7: Common mix-up to avoid. Support: Using the stock record to store signage copy and placement rules makes updates harder and errors more likely.” Increased error likelihood is an unsupported causal claim.
- **Email, body:** “In museum shops, two useful documents often get blended together, and it creates quiet friction.” The asserted common practice is undocumented; “quiet friction” alone is qualitative language.
- **Repurpose pack, nested X post:** “Keeping them separate protects accuracy and keeps the floor clearer.” This repeats the unsupported accuracy claim.
- **Hooks, item 2:** “Two documents museum shops often merge by accident: the stock record and the display note.” Both frequency and accidental cause are invented.
- **Hooks, item 5:** “A clean stock file cannot hold every signage and placement decision. That is what a display note is for.” Different purposes do not establish that storing such information is impossible.

Script cues and carousel outline labels are appropriate to their formats; the brief also expressly permits a neutral no-CTA explanation.

“Two documents. Two jobs.” and the repeated “lanes” metaphor offer little angle variety. Repurposed assets largely duplicate standalone copy. Vague lines such as “feel slightly off,” “feel intentional” or “makes both weaker” are treated as subjective rhetoric, not critical factual claims. They can still be edited for precision.
